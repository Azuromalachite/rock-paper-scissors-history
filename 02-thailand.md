# 2. When did Thai or Siamese people first play it?

**No first occurrence, first introduction or securely dated pre-1939 Siamese
record was found.** That is a limit of the evidence found, not evidence that
people in Siam did not play it.

## The World War II hypothesis

The 2008 article is speculative: Thailand *probably* received the game from
Japan during World War II, *perhaps* by watching Japanese people play. It cites
no wartime diary, newspaper, school record or named eyewitness. Treat it as an
origin hypothesis, not a historical finding.

There may also have been several introductions. A game can circulate through
different communities, take different names, and later become nationally
familiar. Nothing found justifies assuming every Thai version descends from
one introduction in Bangkok.

## Does the word เป่ายิ้งฉุบ reveal its origin?

Not reliably, on the evidence found.

A popular explanation derives it from Chinese words for wrapping/paper,
scissors and hammer. One recent article mixes Mandarin romanisations with
proposed Cantonese pronunciations and gives no historical sequence for the Thai
sound changes. It is a possible lead, not an established etymology or proof of
a Chinese route.

The same caution applies to deriving Thai chants from Japanese because some
syllables sound alike. The history of a game's name and the history of its
rules can differ.
