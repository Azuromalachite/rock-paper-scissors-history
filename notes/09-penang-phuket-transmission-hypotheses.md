# 9. Penang–Phuket: what is evidence for the game, and what is only context

The glass–stone–water version reported from Phuket (and cup/glass from Krabi)
has a close Malaysian parallel. The research handoff of 2026-11-06 proposed a
**Penang–Phuket** route. This note keeps the evidence *about the game* separate
from the evidence *about contact between the places*. The second makes the
first plausible but is not evidence for it.

## Evidence about the game

| strand | what it shows | access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Michael Wong, "Malaysia in Our Classroom: Activities", in *Malaysia/Singapore: Where Asian Cultures Meet* (Fulbright-Hays Seminars Abroad 2001 participants' papers, 2002), ERIC ED471818, printed p. 461, Game #4 | "One Two Som", also called "Hic Haec Hoc". Two players hide their hands behind their heads and chant before revealing. **Glass** (outstretched fingers close together), **water** (open palm), **stone** (fist): glass contains water, water drowns stone, stone breaks glass. **No locality is named**, so it says nothing about Penang. It calls the game ancient, which is a claim, not a date. | read; re-checked by the author |
| A forum thread on Penang Hokkien, January 2005 (chineselanguage.org, t=1611) | Reported by the handoff: recollections of *som tri* / *one two som* with cup, sea or water, and stone, discussing whether it is a Penang or a Hokkien/English-school thing | **not opened**: the site returns errors, and no usable archive copy exists |
| Phuket and Krabi reports | glass–stone–water (Phuket), cup/glass–stone–water (Krabi), under a วันตูโส้ม-type name | one Facebook post and its comments, held privately (see [03](03-southern-thailand.md)) |
| 2009 ASEAN compilation, Malaysia section | One Two Som with **bird**, water, stone | read; see [04](04-malay-peninsula.md) |

So, by 2002, glass–water–stone under the name One Two Som is documented for
Malaysia, without a place. The Penang link rests on one forum thread nobody
has re-opened. The Phuket link rests on private screenshots.

## Context about the places

Khoo Salma Nasution (2009), "Hokkien Chinese on the Phuket Mining Frontier:
The Penang Connection and the Emergence of the Phuket Baba Community",
*Journal of the Malaysian Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society* 82(2),
describes nineteenth-century commercial, migration and kinship links between
Penang and Phuket. Its bibliographic details come from search results and
have not been checked. The Thailand Cultural Encyclopedia's note on Malay and
English words reaching Phuket through Hokkien speakers who had lived in Malaya
points the same way (see [03](03-southern-thailand.md)).

A documented contact network is **not** a documented game transmission. Edges
on any future map must stand for recorded contacts, not assumed game routes.

## Hypotheses to keep apart

1. **Penang → Phuket** with the Hokkien/Baba network.
2. **Phuket → Penang**, or both inheriting it from an earlier peninsular
   repertoire.
3. **Repeated exchange** through schools, trade and family visits, so that
   there is no single route.
4. **Local reinterpretation of one hand shape.** Fingers held close together
   can be read as a **bird**, a **cup** or a **glass** while the cycle stays the
   same. That would explain bird–water–stone and glass–water–stone as one
   gesture with two meanings. It is a hypothesis about gestures, and says
   nothing about which reading came first.

## What would discriminate them

| question | evidence that would count | not enough |
| --- | --- | --- |
| which direction? | dated pre-2002 texts or illustrations; recollections with birth year and place of learning on both sides | matching modern names plus known migration |
| bird → glass, or glass → bird? | earlier sign descriptions; filmed demonstrations by cohort; local explanations | the plausibility of either reading |
| is *one two som* English "one, two"? | early spellings, sound histories, rhythm of the chant across communities | resemblance to "one, two" or to Japanese *jan-ken-pon* |

Before collecting, write down what would count **against** a Penang–Phuket
route. For example: widely different hand shapes under the same name, or Phuket
players who learned it with no Penang contact in their families.
