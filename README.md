# Rock–paper–scissors history: Siam, southern Thailand and the Malay Peninsula

Research notes on the history of rock–paper–scissors (Thai: เป่ายิ้งฉุบ), built
around two questions:

1. When did Thai/Siamese people first play rock–paper–scissors?
2. Why does southern Thailand play bird–stone–water (นก–หิน–น้ำ), glass–stone–water
   and other variants instead?

## Short answers

- **First Thai/Siamese occurrence: not identified.** The earliest dated Thai
  description found is from 1989 (the Thai Junior Encyclopedia, date not yet
  checked against print). Nothing earlier was found. The often-repeated claim
  that Japan introduced the game during World War II is an unsourced
  hypothesis, circulating by 2008, not a documented finding.
- **The southern variants are real and locally distinct.** Reported versions:
  bird–stone–water (Hat Yai/Songkhla), bird–gun–water (Trang, Nakhon Si
  Thammarat), and the standard scissors–hammer–paper (Mueang Surat Thani), from
  dated 2008 online comments. Glass–stone–water (Phuket), cup/glass–stone–water
  (Krabi) and bird–gun–water (Phatthalung) come from one Facebook post and its
  comments, held privately as screenshots and not independently checkable.
- **The strongest explanation is a shared peninsular tradition** with
  Malaysia. A 2009 ASEAN compilation documents Malaysian *One Two Som* with
  bird, water and stone, matching the southern Thai symbols. The Hat Yai chant
  วัน ทู ส้ม, which would match the name too, rests on a single 2008 online
  comment that has not been re-located. The route and direction of
  transmission remain unknown.

## How to read these notes

Each claim is labelled by what kind of evidence supports it: a dated document,
a retrospective recollection, an informal online testimony, or an
interpretation. These are kept separate on purpose. "A game is attested in
place X in year Y" is not the same as "the game arrived in place X in year Y".

## Status and caveats

- These are working research notes, compiled with AI-assisted literature
  searching in Thai, English, Japanese, Chinese and Malay sources. Sources are
  cited by name; links and page numbers have **not** all been independently
  checked (see [open findings](docs/open-findings.md) OF-1 and OF-2). Verify
  against the original before citing.
- Some evidence comes from screenshots of a Facebook post and its comments
  (Phuket/Krabi variants). Those are not reproduced here.
- Corrections, dated sources and oral histories are welcome via issues.
